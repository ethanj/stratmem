# Why Now?

==The shift in global conflict toward Electronic Warfare (EW) and Anti-Access/Area Denial (A2/AD) means the old way of communicating is dead.==

## Three converging waves

### 1. Adversaries can jam now
- Russia's EW corps in Ukraine has demonstrated tactical jamming of GPS, drones, and tactical radios at scale.
- China's PLA invests heavily in **A2/AD** (long-range missiles + EW) specifically to deny U.S. C2 in the Western Pacific.
- The assumption of "we'll always have the cloud" is gone. The DoD knows it.

### 2. SLMs finally fit on a phone
- Gemma 4 E4B + INT4 quantization + Apple NPU = real on-device inference at 30s audio in ~0.3s.
- This wasn't possible 18 months ago. The hardware-software stack just crossed the line.
- ==The "AI on the edge" pitch is no longer aspirational — it's a shipping demo.==

### 3. DoD has formally embraced disaggregation
- DARPA's **Mosaic Warfare** doctrine is funded.
- SBIR/STTR throughput has grown — small-business pathways are mature.
- Programs like **AFWERX**, **DIU**, and **xTechSearch** are explicitly looking for software-first, dual-use, attritable systems. TacNet is the archetype.

## Why nobody else is doing this
- Primes (Lockheed, Raytheon, L3Harris) sell expensive proprietary radios — TacNet cannibalizes that revenue.
- Big-tech defense plays (Anduril, Palantir, Shield AI) are focused on autonomy, ISR, and platforms — not the comms layer beneath them.
- ==The gap is the **AI-native, software-only comms substrate**. That's the lane.==

## TacNet is the bridge
- From: monolithic, satellite-tethered, RF-loud, cognitive-overload C2.
- To: disaggregated, offline-capable, semantic-compressed, AI-augmented C2.
